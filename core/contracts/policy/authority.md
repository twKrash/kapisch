# Human authority

Human decisions, reviewer approval, and side-effect authorization are distinct. `Gate.HUMAN_DECISION` records an explicit human choice; `Gate.APPROVAL` is independent reviewer approval and does not require or imply a second human approval; `Gate.SIDE_EFFECT` records separate human permission for an authorized effect. Explicit human approval of a plan is a `Gate.HUMAN_DECISION` bound to that plan, not a consequence of reviewer approval.

A human gate requires either a host/adapter-produced inbound-human receipt or an explicitly externally supplied human-authored artifact. The receipt itself must bind the observed action to `run_id`, `gate`, `decision_id`, exact `target`, `scope_digest`, and `text_digest`, as well as session-local `action_id`, `session_id`, `origin`, and observation time. Repeating those fields in a controller-owned envelope does not replace the receipt bindings; Stage 5 validation checks that both agree. The external artifact is bound to its exact bytes and the same run, gate, decision, target, and scope. The controller transports and persists these inputs; it must not create either evidence source from model or controller text.

A receipt records observable origin, not cryptographic identity. A digest binds bytes, not authorship; do not claim stronger guarantees. The human owns the decision input, the host adapter owns receipt provenance, and the controller alone serializes an immutable accepted snapshot after that explicit choice. The reviewer owns review judgment; the adapter owns observed invocation facts; the controller binds but never authors those external facts. Accepted snapshots are separate immutable artifacts with explicit governing-source dependencies and amendment/supersession edges. Human acceptance cannot override an active normative source. Changed dependencies stale promotion/execution until re-evaluation and fresh human decision; historical accepted snapshots remain unchanged.
